This one matters most

Of the four documents, this is the one parents actually look for, and the one that cannot carry a placeholder into production. Two questions decide the whole page: are consultants background-checked, and is there a named person responsible for safeguarding?

If the answer to either is currently no, the honest move is to fix the practice and then publish, rather than publish wording that implies otherwise. A false safeguarding claim is far more damaging than an absent page.

Our commitment

The welfare of the students we work with comes before every commercial consideration. We expect every consultant to act in a child's best interests, to keep clear boundaries, and to report any concern immediately rather than wait to be certain.

Who this covers

This statement applies to everyone who works with students on our behalf — consultants, tutors, course leaders and administrative staff — in every setting, online or in person.

Recruitment and vetting

Every consultant is selected on academic record and assessed before being matched with a student.

Needed from you

State exactly what checks are carried out before a consultant works with a child, and do not overstate them. Typically:

  • identity and right-to-work verification;
  • a criminal record check — an enhanced DBS check with the children's barred list in the UK, or the local equivalent elsewhere — and whether it is required of every consultant or only of those working in person;
  • references taken up and verified;
  • a signed code of conduct.

Note that a UK enhanced DBS check is only available where the role meets the statutory definition of regulated activity; an adviser can confirm whether yours does.

How sessions are conducted

  • Consultants work with students in a professional capacity only, and do not form personal relationships with them outside it.
  • Physical contact, gifts and social invitations are not appropriate and are not permitted.
  • Consultants do not meet students at private residences without a parent or guardian present [confirm this reflects your practice for in-person tutoring].
  • Any concern about a student's welfare is reported the same day.

Online sessions

  • Sessions take place on [name the platform] using an account provided by us, not a consultant's personal account.
  • Parents and guardians are welcome to be present, or in the room, at any session.
  • [State your position on recording: whether sessions are recorded, who consents, where recordings are stored and how long they are kept. If sessions are not recorded, say so plainly.]

Communication with students

  • Contact with a student under 18 goes through channels the parent or guardian can see, and through accounts we provide.
  • Consultants do not contact students on personal phone numbers or social media accounts.
  • Messages are limited to the subject of the engagement.

Raising a concern

If you are worried about the welfare of a student, or about the conduct of anyone working with us, tell us straight away.

Needed from you

Name a designated safeguarding lead and give a direct email address and telephone number for them. A generic support address is not adequate for this purpose.

The page should also state that where a child is at immediate risk, the reader should contact the emergency services or their local children's social care service first, and us second — and give the relevant numbers for the countries you operate in.

We will acknowledge a concern within [timeframe] and act on it. We will not penalise anyone — student, parent or consultant — for raising one in good faith.

Records and confidentiality

Safeguarding records are kept separately from ordinary engagement records, with access limited to those who need it. We share information about a concern only with those who need to know, or where we are required to by law. How we handle personal information generally is set out in our Privacy Policy.

Review

This statement is reviewed at least annually, and immediately after any safeguarding incident.
